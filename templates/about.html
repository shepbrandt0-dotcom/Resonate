Why

Why this work matters to me

I love communication. I love people. The two are not separate for me. Words are how we include someone, persuade someone, or leave them outside the room without meaning to. My mom has always said, “Communication is a lifelong struggle.” That line stuck because it is true. Getting heard across age, experience, and culture is never finished work. It is practice.

The struggle shows up in the data, not only in living rooms and campaigns.

On turnout, younger Americans still lag the older electorate in participation. CIRCLE at Tufts estimated that about 47% of youth ages 18 to 29 voted in the 2024 presidential election, below the 2020 youth peak and far from full participation. In the 2022 midterms, CIRCLE estimated that only about 18% of 18- and 19-year-olds voted, compared with 24% of youth ages 20 to 29. Looking ahead, The Civics Center reported that roughly 42% of 18-year-olds were registered for the 2026 midterms in the states they measured, versus about 82% of voters ages 45 to 79. When nearly half of an eligible generation is missing from the conversation, the words leaders use either open the door or quietly close it.

Intergenerational misunderstanding is measurable at work too. An Indeed survey cited in coverage of multigenerational teams found that different communication styles and expectations across generations ranked among the top challenges in mixed-age workplaces, with poor workplace communication estimated to cost companies on the order of tens of billions of dollars a year in the United States. A Korn Ferry survey of thousands of employees found that only 17% of Gen Z workers said they had no problem working across generations, compared with 45% of baby boomers. The gap is not only preference. It is whether people feel understood.

That is why Resonate exists. I care about the craft of speaking so another generation can actually hear it. Campaigns and public figures who want younger voters in the room need language that respects how those voters listen, without pretending to be something they are not. I love the people on both sides of that exchange enough to treat the words carefully.

Sources

  • CIRCLE (Tufts): youth turnout estimates for 2024 (~47% ages 18–29); 2022 midterms (~18% ages 18–19 vs ~24% ages 20–29).
  • The Civics Center: ~42% of 18-year-olds registered (Spring 2026, multi-state files) vs ~82% ages 45–79.
  • Indeed survey (reported in Entrepreneur): generational communication styles as a top multigenerational workplace challenge; poor communication cost estimates in the tens of billions annually.
  • Korn Ferry (reported in Fortune, 2025): 17% of Gen Z vs 45% of baby boomers report no difficulty working across generations.
